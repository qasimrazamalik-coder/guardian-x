# How laptop batteries wear out

## What ages a lithium-ion battery
A lithium-ion battery loses capacity in two ways. Calendar ageing happens with time, and it is fastest when the battery sits at a high charge (near 100%) and when it is warm. Cycle ageing comes from charging and discharging. For a gaming laptop that stays plugged in, calendar ageing usually dominates: the battery spends hours at 100% while the CPU and GPU heat the chassis.

## Why keeping it plugged in all the time is not ideal
When a laptop stays on the charger it usually holds the battery at or near 100%. High state of charge plus heat speeds up the side reactions that permanently reduce capacity. Keeping a gaming laptop plugged in is fine for performance, but for battery health it is better to avoid sitting at 100% for many hours, keep it cool, and let it run down to around 40-60% from time to time. Where a charge limit exists (80%), it is the single most effective protection. On HP OMEN laptops there is no built-in charge limit, which is why Guardian X can use a smart plug to create one.

## Charge cycles
One cycle means 100% of the capacity used, possibly across several partial discharges (for example 50% twice). Partial cycles in the middle of the range (for example 30% to 80%) are gentler than full 0-100% cycles. Many laptop batteries are rated to keep about 80% of their capacity after roughly 300 to 1000 cycles, depending on the maker.

## Wear speed
Wear per 100 cycles separates "used a lot" from "ageing badly". A battery rated to keep 80% after 500 cycles loses about 4% per 100 cycles. Losing much more than that per 100 cycles means most of the wear comes from time spent full, heat, or both, not from use.

## Deep discharge
Running the battery to near 0% regularly also stresses it. Plugging in around 20% is a good habit. Very low charge for long periods (days) is harmful.

## Heat
Heat is the other main cause of fast wear. Gaming with the laptop on a bed or soft surface blocks the vents. A cooling pad, a hard flat surface, clean vents and moderate performance modes reduce battery temperature. Many OMEN batteries do not report their temperature to Windows, so Guardian X cannot measure it directly on those models.

## Capacity readings and calibration
Windows reports full charge capacity from the battery's fuel gauge. The value moves up and down a little from day to day; that is normal. A long-term downward trend is what matters. Occasionally letting the battery run from full to around 10% helps the gauge stay accurate.

## Storage
For storing a laptop for weeks, leave the battery around 40-60% and keep it cool.
